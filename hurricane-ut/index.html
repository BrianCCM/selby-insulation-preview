Why Hurricane Homes Need Quality Insulation

Hurricane sits at roughly 3,200 feet in the warmest corner of the state. The summer is long, the sun is direct, and the air is dry enough that very little of the overnight cool is held onto. Four things follow from that, and all of them land on your insulation.

Attic temperatures reach 150 degrees

July highs in Hurricane average close to 100 degrees, and under dark shingles in that sun an attic commonly reaches 140 to 150 degrees by mid afternoon. A ceiling with thin or settled insulation holds that heat against your drywall well into the evening.

Most ductwork runs through the attic

Supply ducts in homes here typically run through the attic space. Air travelling through a 150 degree attic reaches the registers warmer than it left the air handler, so part of the cooling you are paying for is gone before it arrives. Insulating and air sealing the attic is usually the least expensive way to correct that.

Temperatures swing in both directions

A 102 degree afternoon can drop into the 60s overnight, and January mornings fall close to freezing. Insulation in this climate is not working against summer heat alone. It is doing a job in every season.

Rain arrives in short, heavy storms

Most of the year is dry, and then monsoon storms move through in late summer and drop a significant amount of rain in a short period. Closed cell spray foam resists moisture in the assemblies most exposed to it, including rim joists and roof decks.

Insulation Services for Hurricane Homes

We install the same products we would anywhere in Southern Utah. What changes in Hurricane is where the material goes and how much of it the climate justifies.

Blown-In Attic Insulation

This is the highest return upgrade on most Hurricane homes. We bring the attic to the R-38 Washington County requires, with consistent depth across the entire ceiling and baffles keeping the soffit vents clear. Can lights, top plates and bath fan penetrations are air sealed first so the new material is not simply covering open leaks.

Spray Foam Insulation

Closed cell foam where an assembly needs air sealing, moisture resistance and high R-value in a shallow cavity, including vaulted roof decks, rim joists and conditioned attics that hold HVAC equipment. Open cell for interior partitions and sound control. We will tell you which one your home actually needs rather than defaulting to the more expensive option.

Fiberglass Insulation

Walls, floors, garage ceilings and the common wall between an attached garage and the house. Garages in Hurricane regularly pass 110 degrees in August, and that heat moves into the rooms next to them through an uninsulated wall. We install Owens Corning, CertainTeed and Johns Manville products.

Insulation Removal

Many homes in the area still have their original insulation, compressed over the years to a fraction of its rated depth. Water damage, smoke and rodent contamination are the other common reasons to remove it. We vacuum the old material out, clean the space and reinstall the same day where the job allows.

Crawl Space and Subfloor

Insulation under the floor, rim joist air sealing, and full encapsulation when the crawl space needs to be brought inside the thermal envelope.

Home Energy Assessment

Before you commit to any work, we can measure where the heat is getting in. Our assessment includes a blower door test, thermal imaging analysis, air leak detection, an HVAC system evaluation, an insulation inspection and a review of your energy bills.

Insulation Requirements in Washington County

Hurricane is in climate zone 3B, the hot dry one, so Washington County requires R-38 in the ceiling. Iron, Beaver and Garfield County to the north require R-49. The figures below are the prescriptive values for new construction, and they are a reasonable target for an older home you are bringing up to date.

Correctly installed insulation generally lasts 15 to 20 years. Homes built around here before 2010 are often sitting at R-19 or R-30 in the attic, and bringing a ceiling up to R-38 is usually a single day of work.

Frequently Asked Questions

How much does attic insulation cost in Hurricane?

Cost depends on square footage, the condition of what is already up there, and whether it needs to come out first. Adding to a clean attic on a single story home costs considerably less than a removal and reinstallation on a two story home with a complicated roofline. We give you a firm price before any work starts, and the estimate is free and usually available the same day you call.

Should the old insulation be removed, or can we add to it?

If the existing material is dry, clean and evenly distributed, we can usually install over it and count the existing R-value toward the total. It does need to come out if it is wet, damaged, smoke affected or contaminated by rodents, because installing over it can cause further damage. We will assess what is there and recommend the appropriate approach.

Is blown-in or spray foam better for a Hurricane attic?

On a home with ductwork in a vented attic, blown-in insulation to R-38 combined with thorough air sealing delivers the most improvement for the money. Spray foam applied to the underside of the roof deck makes sense when the attic needs to be brought inside the conditioned space, when a vaulted ceiling has no room for depth, or when duct losses are severe enough to justify the cost. We will walk the attic and tell you which applies.

How long does the work take?

Most attic projects are finished in a single day. A removal and reinstallation usually also fits in one day, occasionally two on a larger home. Spray foam needs cure time before other trades return to the space, and we will give you the exact interval when we schedule.

Will new insulation reduce my power bill?

On a home that is currently under-insulated, yes, and the comfort improvement is usually what people notice first. Rooms stop varying by ten degrees and the air conditioner stops running flat out all afternoon. The exact dollar amount depends on your equipment, your windows and how you run the thermostat, so we will not quote you a figure we cannot stand behind.

What is an R-value?

R-value measures how well a material resists heat transfer. The higher the number, the better the product insulates. Washington County requires R-38 in the ceiling of a new home, and most homes in Hurricane benefit from reaching that number whether or not they were originally built to it.
